Diabetes Care Pathway — Key Performance Indicators & Training Needs

National monitoring framework and workforce capacity building across primary, secondary, and tertiary care levels in Sri Lanka.

PRIMARY

1. Primary Care Level

Institutions: Arogya Centres, District Hospitals Type A (DH-A), District Hospitals Type B (DH-B), District Hospitals Type C (DH-C), Medical Clinics of the Primary Institution
Role: First-contact care, screening, diagnosis, initial management, health promotion, disease prevention, stable patient follow-up

1. Screening Coverage (≥50%)
All adults ≥35 years with risk factors (BMI ≥23 kg/m², family history, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, previous GDM, physical inactivity, smoking) should be screened at least annually using FPG, OGTT, or HbA1c. Coverage is calculated as the proportion of the eligible registered population that receives screening in a calendar year.
2. HbA1c Control (≥60% <7%)
Among all diagnosed Type 2 diabetes patients on the register, at least 60% should achieve HbA1c below 7% (53 mmol/mol) at their most recent measurement. This reflects the quality of glycaemic management at primary care level.
3. Blood Pressure Control (≥70% <130/80)
Among all registered diabetes patients, at least 70% should have blood pressure below 130/80 mmHg, in line with ADA and CPG 2025 targets for comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction.
4. Foot Examination (≥80% annual)
At least 80% of registered diabetes patients should receive a documented annual foot examination including 10g monofilament testing, 128Hz tuning fork, and visual inspection for ulcers or deformities.
5. Retinal Screening (≥80% annual)
At least 80% of registered diabetes patients should be referred for and complete retinal screening annually, either via direct ophthalmoscopy or fundus photography.
6. Referral Timeliness (100% within 2 weeks)
All patients with newly detected diabetes complications, uncontrolled diabetes (HbA1c >9%), or diagnostic uncertainty should be referred to secondary care within 2 weeks of detection.
7. NCD Register Coverage (100%)
Every patient diagnosed with diabetes in the catchment area must be enrolled in the NCD screening register with a unique identifier to enable longitudinal tracking and continuum of care.
8. BMI Documentation (≥90%)
Body mass index and waist circumference should be documented in at least 90% of all patient encounters to enable monitoring of metabolic targets and treatment response.

SECONDARY

2. Secondary Care Level

Institutions: Base Hospitals Type A (BH-A), Base Hospitals Type B (BH-B)
Role: Specialist assessment, complication management, consultant review, advanced diagnostics, nutritional care, eyecare, footcare, MDT coordination, counter-referral of stable patients

1. Specialist Waiting Time (Routine ≤2 weeks; Urgent ≤48 hours)
The time from receipt of referral to first specialist appointment. Routine referrals include stable patients with complications; urgent referrals include acute foot infection, rapidly deteriorating vision, or metabolic decompensation.
2. Complication Review Rate (≥90%)
Of all patients referred from primary care for specialist assessment, at least 90% should be seen by a consultant (physician, endocrinologist, or designated diabetes specialist) and receive a documented management plan.
3. HbA1c Improvement (≥50% with ≥0.5% reduction)
Among patients referred with suboptimal glycaemic control (HbA1c >8%), at least 50% should demonstrate a clinically meaningful reduction of at least 0.5% (5.5 mmol/mol) within 6 months of specialist intervention.
4. DR Treatment Timeliness (100% within 2 weeks)
All patients diagnosed with sight-threatening diabetic retinopathy (proliferative DR, clinically significant macular oedema) must receive laser photocoagulation or intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy within 2 weeks of diagnosis.
5. Foot Ulcer Healing Rate (≥70% at 12 weeks)
Among patients presenting with diabetic foot ulcers, at least 70% should demonstrate a reduction in wound surface area by week 12, reflecting the effectiveness of wound care protocols including debridement, offloading, and infection management.
6. CV Risk Assessment (100%)
Every diabetes patient attending secondary care must have a comprehensive cardiovascular risk assessment documented, including lipid profile, renal function, ECG, and use of WHO/ISH risk charts to guide statin and aspirin therapy.
7. Inpatient Mortality (<5%)
The proportion of diabetes-related hospital admissions (DKA, HHS, foot sepsis, stroke, ACS) resulting in in-hospital death should be below 5%, reflecting the quality of acute management.
8. Counter-Referral Rate (≥40% within 6 months)
At least 40% of patients whose condition stabilises after specialist intervention should be formally counter-referred back to primary care with a clear management plan, ensuring efficient use of specialist capacity and continuity of care.

TERTIARY

3. Tertiary Care Level

Institutions: National Hospitals (NH), Provincial General Hospitals (PGH), Teaching Hospitals (TH), District General Hospitals (DGH)
Role: Complex case management, specialised procedures (transplant, revascularisation), critical care, advanced rehabilitation, training of lower-level staff, research, programme leadership

1. Complex Case Resolution (≥85%)
Of all patients referred to tertiary care with complex or refractory diabetes (Type 1 diabetes, monogenic diabetes, steroid-induced diabetes, ketosis-prone diabetes, recurrent DKA, brittle diabetes), at least 85% should receive a definitive specialist management plan within 4 weeks of first appointment.
2. Amputation Prevention (≥90%)
Among patients classified as high-risk for major amputation (severe peripheral arterial disease, Charcot neuroarthropathy, non-healing ulcers with osteomyelitis), at least 90% should avoid below-knee or above-knee amputation through vascular intervention, advanced wound care, and multidisciplinary management.
3. Renal Transplant Survival (≥85% at 1 year)
For diabetic patients receiving renal transplantation (simultaneous kidney-pancreas or kidney-alone), the 1-year graft survival rate should be at least 85%, benchmarked against international transplant registries.
4. Emergency Survival Rate (≥95%)
Among patients admitted to ICU with diabetic emergencies (severe DKA, HHS, hypoglycaemic coma), at least 95% should survive to ICU discharge, reflecting the quality of critical care management.
5. Training Output (≥2 sessions annually)
Each tertiary centre must conduct at least 2 structured training sessions per year for primary and secondary care staff, including clinical updates, skills workshops, and case discussion forums.
6. Research Output (≥1 publication per unit)
Each tertiary diabetes unit should contribute at least one peer-reviewed publication per year to the national and international evidence base, supporting quality improvement and policy development.
7. Patient Satisfaction (≥85%)
Patient satisfaction scores for specialist diabetes services, measured through structured surveys covering access, communication, clinical care, and coordination, should be at least 85%.
8. MDT Completion Rate (100%)
Every complex case (as defined by the tertiary referral criteria) must be formally discussed at a multidisciplinary team meeting with documented attendance from relevant specialties (endocrinology, nephrology, ophthalmology, vascular surgery, podiatry, nutrition).
